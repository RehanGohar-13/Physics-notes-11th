Part 1 · Foundations
1 Physical Quantities & Units
Key Terms
Physical Quantity Base Units (SI) Derived Units 7 Base Quantities

Physical Quantity

Definition: Any quantity that can be measured and consists of a numerical value and a unit (e.g., 5 kg).

Base Units (SI)

Fundamental, independent quantities that cannot be defined in terms of other units.

  • Length — metre (m)
  • Mass — kilogram (kg)
  • Time — second (s)
  • Temperature — kelvin (K)
  • Electric Current — ampere (A)
  • Amount of Substance — mole (mol)
  • Luminous Intensity — candela (cd)

Derived Units

Formed by combinations of base units.

  • Velocity — m/s
  • Force — kg·m/s² (or N)
Summary

Physics is built on 7 SI base units. Every other unit (derived) is a combination of these. A complete physical quantity always has a number and a unit.

3 Uncertainty & Significant Figures
Key Terms
Accuracy Precision Systematic Error Random Error Significant Figures

Accuracy vs. Precision

🎯 Accuracy

How close a measurement is to the true or accepted value.

🔁 Precision

The closeness of repeated measurements to each other.

Types of Errors

  • Systematic Errors — Shift all readings in one direction.
  • Random Errors — Unpredictable scatter in measurements.

Significant Figures

Definition: All accurately known digits plus the first uncertain digit.
  • Non-zero digits are always significant.
  • Zeros between non-zero digits are significant.
  • Leading zeros are not significant.
Summary

Accuracy ≠ Precision. Significant figures communicate measurement reliability.

4 Dimensions of Physical Quantities
Key Terms
Dimensions [M], [L], [T] Dimensional Analysis Homogeneity

Dimensions

Definition: The dependence of a physical quantity on base quantities, represented as powers of Mass [M], Length [L], and Time [T].

Dimensional Analysis

  • Check homogeneity of equations.
  • Derive relations between quantities.

Example: Force

Force = Mass × Acceleration
[F] = [M1 L1 T−2]
Summary

Dimensions reduce quantities to [M], [L], [T]. If dimensions don't match, the equation is definitely wrong.

Part 2 · Errors, Uncertainty & Advanced
5 Precision and Accuracy
Key Terms
Accuracy Precision Least Count Zero Error

Accuracy

Definition: The degree of closeness between a measured value and the true / standard value. High accuracy = little to no systematic error.

Precision

Definition: The degree of exactness or reproducibility — how close repeated measurements are to one another. Determined by the least count.
Key Distinction: A measurement can be very precise (e.g., repeating 5.123 cm) but inaccurate if the instrument has a zero-error.
Summary

Accuracy = closeness to truth; Precision = closeness to each other. Always check for calibration issues.

6 Uncertainty and Types of Errors
Key Terms
Uncertainty (Δx) Systematic Error Random Error Parallax

Uncertainty (Δx)

Definition: The doubt around any measurement, expressed as an absolute value alongside it.
L = 5.0 ± 0.1 cm

Systematic Errors

  • Shift measurements in one direction consistently.
  • Causes: Zero-errors, faulty calibration, environmental factors.
  • Fix: Recalibrate instruments, correct technique.

Random Errors

  • Fluctuate randomly around the true value.
  • Causes: Parallax, fluctuating conditions.
  • Fix: Multiple trials → take the average.
Summary

Every measurement has uncertainty. Systematic = one-direction bias (recalibrate). Random = scatter (average out).

7 Error Propagation
Key Terms
Error Propagation Absolute Uncertainty Fractional Uncertainty Powers Rule

When calculated quantities use measured values with uncertainties, those uncertainties propagate:

➕ Addition & Subtraction

Absolute uncertainties are added.

ΔZ = ΔA + ΔB

✖️ Multiplication & Division

Fractional uncertainties are added.

ΔZ/Z = ΔA/A + ΔB/B

🔺 Powers / Exponents

Fractional uncertainty × the power.

ΔZ/Z = n × (ΔA/A)
Summary

Add absolutes for +/−; add fractionals for ×/÷; multiply by exponent for powers.

8 Significant Figures in Calculations
Key Terms
Fewest Sig. Figs. Least Decimal Place Rounding

Multiplication & Division

Result has the same number of sig. figs. as the measurement with the fewest sig. figs.

Addition & Subtraction

Result is rounded to the least number of decimal places.

Intermediate Steps

Golden Rule: Keep at least one extra digit during intermediate steps. Round only at the final step.
Summary

×/÷ → fewest sig. figs. +/− → fewest decimal places. Never round mid-calculation.

9 Advanced Dimensional Analysis
Key Terms
Homogeneity Consistency Check Deriving Relations

Dimensional Homogeneity

Every valid equation must be dimensionally consistent — all terms on both sides share identical dimensions.

Applications

  • Checking Equations: e.g., verifying s = ut + ½at²
  • Deriving Relations: Finding how quantities depend on each other.

Example Dimensions

Quantity Dimension
Length [L]
Mass [M]
Time [T]
Velocity [L T−1]
Acceleration [L T−2]
Force [M L T−2]
Remember: Dimensional analysis can prove an equation wrong, but not right — dimensionless constants (½, π) are invisible to it!
Summary

All terms must have identical dimensions. A fast sanity check, but it ignores dimensionless constants.

Part 3 · Systems, Supplementary & Order of Magnitude
10 Systems of Units
Key Terms
CGS System FPS System MKS System SI System

Four Major Systems

📏 CGS System

Centimetre (cm), Gram (g), Second (s)

🇬🇧 FPS (British)

Foot (ft), Pound (lb), Second (s)

📐 MKS System

Metre (m), Kilogram (kg), Second (s)

🌍 SI System

The globally accepted modern metric system — expands MKS to include 7 base units and 2 supplementary units.

Summary

Four main systems exist: CGS, FPS, MKS, and SI. The SI system is the globally accepted standard, building upon MKS with 7 base + 2 supplementary units.

11 Supplementary Quantities & Units
Key Terms
Plane Angle (θ) Radian (rad) Solid Angle (Ω) Steradian (sr)

Plane Angle (θ)

Unit: Radian (rad)
Definition: Ratio of arc length to radius.
θ = s / r

Solid Angle (Ω)

Unit: Steradian (sr)
Definition: Three-dimensional analogue of an angle, subtended at the center of a sphere by a surface area.
Ω = Area / r²
Summary

Two supplementary units: Radian (plane angle = arc/radius) and Steradian (solid angle = area/radius²). Both are dimensionless ratios.

12 Order of Magnitude
Key Terms
Order of Magnitude Nearest Power of 10 Estimation Rule Standard Form

Definition

Order of Magnitude: The nearest power of 10 that represents the scale or size of a physical quantity.

Estimation Rule

Express any measurement in standard form as A × 10n, where 0.5 < A ≤ 5. The exponent n represents the order of magnitude.

Example

Speed of light = 3 × 108 m/s
Order of magnitude = 108
Why it matters: Orders of magnitude let you quickly compare vastly different scales — from atoms (10−10 m) to galaxies (1021 m) — without worrying about exact numbers.
Summary

Order of magnitude = the power of 10 nearest to a quantity's size. It's a quick estimation tool for comparing scales across physics.
